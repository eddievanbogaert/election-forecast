## Battleground Races

### North Carolina (NC) — Open Seat, PVI R+3
**Rating: Likely D** | Polling avg: D+7 | D win prob: ~76%

Thom Tillis not seeking re-election. Roy Cooper (former two-term governor) is the D nominee — a high-quality recruit with strong statewide name ID. R primary held March 3; Mark Whatley is the nominee. Cooper leads Whatley by 1–10 pts across 7 polls (avg D+7), with the trend moving toward Cooper over time. The TIPP +24 poll was excluded as an outlier. Top Democratic pickup opportunity.

### Georgia (GA) — Ossoff (D), PVI R+4
**Rating: Lean D** | Polling avg: D+5.3 | D win prob: ~70%

Ossoff won by just 1.2% in the January 2021 runoff and is the top Republican target. However, early polling shows him leading all potential R opponents (Collins, Carter, Dooley) by 3–8 pts. The R primary field is still unsettled. Georgia's competitive electorate and Ossoff's incumbency make this Lean D despite the red PVI.

### Alaska (AK) — Sullivan (R), PVI R+9
**Rating: Lean R** | Polling avg: D+2 | D win prob: ~40%

Mary Peltola (former congresswoman who lost her House seat in 2024) is challenging Sullivan. Recent polls show Peltola leading by 1–4 pts despite R+9 PVI, averaged to D+2. Alaska uses ranked-choice voting and has a history of electing independents and crossover candidates. The fundamentals heavily favor Sullivan, but polling suggests this is competitive.

### Maine (ME) — Collins (R), PVI D+3
**Rating: Toss-up** | Polling avg: D+2.5 | D win prob: ~55%

Collins is a moderate Republican who historically outperforms PVI by 10+ points. Key 2026 battleground. D primary is unsettled — Jason Platner leads Collins by 7 in one poll; Collins leads Mills by 2. Blended average of D+2.5. The fundamentals vs. polling tension here reflects Collins's crossover appeal vs. the national environment.

### Michigan (MI) — Open Seat, PVI R+1
**Rating: Toss-up** | No polling yet | D win prob: ~55%

Gary Peters retiring. D primary: State Sen. Mallory McMorrow, Rep. Haley Stevens, and Abdul El-Sayed among leading candidates. Open seat in a true swing state — top-tier battleground. No general election polling yet; model relies on fundamentals (PVI R+1 + national environment D+3.5 shift).

### New Hampshire (NH) — Open Seat, PVI D+1
**Rating: Lean D** | No polling yet | D win prob: ~62%

Jeanne Shaheen retiring. D favorite: Rep. Chris Pappas. R primary: former Sen. John Sununu and former MA Sen. Scott Brown. Highly competitive open seat in a swing state. No polling yet; fundamentals favor D slightly.

### Texas (TX) — Cornyn (R), PVI R+10
**Rating: Safe R** | Polling avg: R+1 | D win prob: ~15%

R primary headed to runoff (Paxton vs Cornyn). D nominee James Talarico polls within 1–2 pts of both R candidates. PPP polls (D-sponsored) show Talarico +2/+1; UH Hobby polls (neutral) show R+1/R+2. Averaged to R+1. The bruising R primary could depress general election turnout. While PVI R+10 is a strong headwind, the polling suggests this is more competitive than a typical TX race. Currently at ~15% D win prob — watchlist race.

### Nebraska (NE) — Ricketts (R), PVI R+22
**Rating: Safe R** | Polling avg: R+1 | D win prob: ~8%

Dan Osborn (Independent) challenging Ricketts, who was appointed in 2023 after Ben Sasse resigned. Osborn nearly beat Deb Fischer in 2024 and is a strong populist candidate. Polls show Ricketts +1 despite R+22 PVI — a remarkable gap. Osborn would likely caucus with Democrats for Senate control. Modeled on D side for control purposes. The fundamentals still dominate at this distance from the election.
